# Hello, I'm Mateo.

I'm a freelance / independent developer who enjoys building things, experimenting with different technologies, and learning through projects.

I don't really have a single area that I focus on. I like exploring different parts of development depending on what I want to build or what I find interesting. Most of my work revolves around web development, applications, servers, artificial intelligence, bots, tools, automation, and other technology-related projects.

Sometimes I start working on something because I have a specific idea in mind. Other times, I simply want to see if I can make something work.

## About Me

Programming is something I mainly do as a hobby, but that doesn't mean I don't take it seriously.

When I have an idea that I consider worth developing, I like putting real time and effort into it. A project can start as a small experiment and eventually become something much larger, depending on how the idea develops and how much potential I see in it.

I also do freelance development, although not everything I build is intended to be a professional or commercial project. A lot of what I do is simply driven by curiosity, experimentation, or the desire to learn something new.

I enjoy figuring things out on my own, especially when I come across something I haven't worked with before. I don't necessarily need to know how something works before starting a project. Sometimes the project itself is how I learn.

## What You'll Find Here

This GitHub contains a collection of things I've worked on over time.

You may find personal projects, experiments, tools, prototypes, applications, server-related projects, AI experiments, automation, and other things that I decided to build.

Not everything here has the same purpose or the same level of development.

Some repositories exist because I wanted to experiment with an idea. Others are projects that required significantly more time and planning. Some may continue to evolve, while others may simply remain as a record of something I built and learned from.

The amount of time, effort, and seriousness I put into a project depends largely on the project itself.

## How I Work

I like experimenting with ideas instead of limiting myself to things I already know.

If I come across a technology, concept, or problem that interests me, I'll usually try to work with it, even if I have little or no previous experience with it.

This means that learning is a natural part of most of my projects.

I don't expect every project to be perfect from the beginning. Some ideas need to be tested before they make sense, and sometimes the best way to understand something is simply to build it.

Because of that, some projects may change considerably during development. An idea that starts one way can end up becoming something completely different after experimenting with it.

## Projects & Experiments

I don't follow a fixed type of project.

One project might be a small tool made to solve a specific problem. Another might be a web application, a server, an AI experiment, an automation system, or something completely different.

I also enjoy taking small ideas and seeing where they can go.

A project doesn't necessarily need to start as something big. Sometimes I build a small version first, and if I think the idea is worth pursuing, I continue developing it and give it more structure, features, and time.

Other times, an experiment simply remains an experiment.

Both are useful.

## About the Code

The repositories here should be considered a reflection of what I was building or learning at the time they were created.

Some projects may be unfinished, experimental, or contain things that I would do differently today. Others may be more complete and receive much more attention.

I don't see that as a problem.

Projects are also a way of documenting progress. Something that may look simple today can represent an important step in learning something new.

As I continue developing, older projects may also show the difference between what I knew then and what I know later.

## Freelance

Alongside my personal projects, I also work on freelance development when there is a project that fits.

The type of work can vary depending on what is needed. I don't limit myself to a single category of development, so the approach and technologies can change from project to project.

For professional projects, I take a more structured approach and focus more heavily on reliability, organization, maintainability, and the requirements of the project.

Personal projects give me more freedom to experiment.

I enjoy having both sides.

## Why I Build

For me, one of the most interesting parts of programming is turning an idea into something that actually works.

An idea can start as a simple thought:

> "What if I made this?"

From there, it can become a small experiment, a prototype, or eventually a complete project.

Sometimes it works exactly as expected.

Sometimes it doesn't.

And sometimes the process of figuring out why it doesn't work is what makes the project worth doing.
